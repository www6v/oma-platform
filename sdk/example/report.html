1. Revenue by Category

Office supplies dominate revenue at 33.0% of the total, driven by high-ticket items like ergonomic chairs and standing desks — despite having only 11 orders (22% of volume). Sports follows at 26.6%, fueled by a single $449 Exercise Bike purchase.

Key takeaway: Office generates the most revenue per order ($123 avg) while Home generates the least ($59 avg). The top 3 products by revenue — Exercise Bike ($449), Ergonomic Chair ($389), and Standing Desk ($299) — together account for 27.6% of all revenue.

2. Revenue by Region

The West region leads with $1,526 in revenue (37.3%), more than double the South ($663). The Midwest punches above its weight at $1,152 — the second-highest region — largely thanks to the $449 Exercise Bike and $160 Printer orders.

3. Category × Region Breakdown

The heatmap reveals significant geographic concentration. Office revenue is entirely concentrated in the West ($877) and South ($390) — the East region has zero Office revenue. Similarly, Electronics has no presence in the South.

Gap alert: Two of the 20 category-region cells show $0 revenue. The East region's complete absence from Office sales and the South's absence from Electronics represent untapped market opportunities.

4. Repeat Customer Behavior

15 of 19 customers (79%) placed more than one order during January. These repeat customers generated $2,883 of the $4,094 total — 70.4% of revenue. The most active customer (C001) placed 5 orders, all in Electronics.

5. 🔍 The Surprising Pattern: Perfect Category Loyalty

Every single repeat customer — all 15 of them — purchased exclusively from one category. Not a single repeat customer crossed category boundaries. This 100% single-category loyalty rate is extraordinarily rare in e-commerce, where cross-selling typically captures 20–40% of repeat purchases.

Surprising finding: The data shows a perfect 15/15 single-category loyalty rate. C003 bought 4 Home items but never ventured into Sports or Electronics. C004 bought 4 Sports items exclusively. C009 spent $877 on 3 Office orders without buying anything else. Customers appear to self-select into a category and never leave it.

6. Top Products by Revenue

Revenue is heavily concentrated in a few high-ticket items. The top 5 products alone account for 39.5% of total revenue. This long-tail distribution means a handful of SKUs drive the majority of the top line.

📋 Actionable Recommendations

  1. Break the category silos with targeted cross-sell campaigns. The 100% single-category loyalty rate is the biggest growth lever. Bundle complementary products across categories (e.g., "Home Office Starter Kit" pairing a Desk Lamp with a Standing Desk) to convert single-category buyers into multi-category shoppers.
  2. Activate the East region for Office sales. Zero Office revenue in the East is a clear gap. Launch a region-specific Office promotion or ensure the product assortment and marketing reach East-region customers who currently only buy Sports and Electronics.
  3. Fill the South's Electronics gap. Similarly, zero Electronics revenue in the South suggests either a distribution or awareness problem. Targeted ads or a South-region Electronics launch could unlock a new revenue stream.
  4. Invest in the West's Office segment. The West already generates $877 in Office revenue — the highest single cell in the heatmap. Doubling down here with premium Office bundles or loyalty rewards for high-spenders like C009 ($877) could yield outsized returns.
  5. Reduce dependency on top-SKU concentration. The top 5 products drive 39.5% of revenue. Diversify demand by promoting mid-tier products and creating category-specific "discovery" promotions to broaden the revenue base.
  6. Build a formal repeat-customer program. With 79% of customers already returning, a structured loyalty program (tiered rewards, early access, category-specific perks) could increase order frequency from the current 2–5 orders/month to sustained engagement.
