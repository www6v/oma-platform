Key Insight

Repeat customers spend 0.4× more per order than one-time buyers. The 9 repeat customers generated $3,285 of the $4,094 total — a classic Pareto-like concentration where less than half of customers drive nearly two-thirds of revenue.

The Surprising Pattern

Surprise

Every single repeat customer stayed within their original category. Of the 15 customers who placed multiple orders, not one crossed category boundaries. C001 bought 5 Electronics items; C002 bought 4 Sports items; C003 bought 3 Home items. Category loyalty among repeat buyers is 100%.

This is striking because cross-category purchasing is the norm in most e-commerce datasets. Here, customers behave as if each category were a separate store. The implication: cross-sell campaigns that recommend products from different categories may underperform, while within-category upsells (accessories, bundles, upgrades) have a much higher probability of converting.

Daily Revenue Trend

Revenue is lumpy — driven by occasional big-ticket purchases (Exercise Bike $449, Ergonomic Chair $389, Standing Desk $299) rather than steady daily flow. The 7-day rolling average reveals a generally flat trend with no clear growth or decline signal in six weeks of data.

Actionable Recommendations

  • Launch a repeat-buyer loyalty program. With 79% of customers already returning and generating 80% of revenue, formalizing this behavior with points, early access, or subscription discounts could lift retention and AOV further.
  • Invest in within-category upsells, not cross-category cross-sells. The 100% category loyalty among repeat buyers means "Customers who bought X also bought Y" recommendations should stay inside the same category. Bundle accessories with Electronics, premium mats with Sports gear, etc.
  • Expand Electronics in the South and Office in the East. Both region–category cells show $0 revenue — untapped demand or missing marketing. Targeted campaigns or regional inventory allocation could unlock new revenue with minimal risk.
  • Reduce dependence on big-ticket spikes. The top 3 orders ($449 + $389 + $299 = $1,137) account for 28% of total revenue. Diversify with mid-range product introductions ($100–$200) in Home and Sports to smooth revenue volatility.
  • Deepen the Midwest opportunity. With only 11 orders but $1,152 in revenue (highest AOV at $104.72), the Midwest shows strong per-customer value. Increase marketing spend or regional promotions to grow order volume without sacrificing AOV.
