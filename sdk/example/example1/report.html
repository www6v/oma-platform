1. Revenue by Category

Office supplies dominate despite fewer transactions

Office products generated $1,352 (33% of total revenue) from just 11 orders — the highest average order value at $123. Sports follows at $1,089 from 14 orders, while Home products trail at $654.

Key insight: Office's revenue leadership is driven by high-ticket furniture (Standing Desk $299, Ergonomic Chair $389, Bookshelf $189). A single product category — office furniture — accounts for 20% of all revenue.

2. Revenue by Region

West leads; Midwest punches above its weight

The West region leads with $1,526 across 15 orders, fueled by both high-ticket office purchases and consistent electronics demand.

The Midwest, despite only 11 orders, generated $1,152 — the highest average order value of any region at $105. This is driven by a single $449 Exercise Bike purchase and concentrated Home + Office spending.

Key insight: The East region has the most orders (14) but the lowest revenue ($753), with an AOV of just $54 — nearly half the Midwest's. East customers buy lower-priced items in small quantities.

3. Category–Region Cross-Analysis

Revenue heatmap reveals sharp regional specialization

Office revenue is heavily concentrated in the West ($877) and South ($390), while Electronics are absent from the South entirely. Home products show a similar gap — zero revenue in the South.

4. 🔍 The Surprising Pattern: Regional Category Deserts

Some regions completely ignore entire product categories
Surprising finding: The South region has zero Electronics revenue and zero Home revenue — 100% of its spending goes to Sports and Office. Meanwhile, the East has zero Office revenue despite being the second-largest region by order count. These aren't small gaps — they're complete category deserts covering half the product catalog.

This pattern suggests either (a) regional marketing is not reaching the right audiences, (b) regional fulfillment/shipping costs deter certain purchases, or (c) customer acquisition in these regions is channel-biased. The South, for example, has 10 orders but has never bought a single electronic product or home item — a significant untapped opportunity given that Electronics averages $71/order nationally.

If the South matched the national average for Electronics share (24%), it would represent ~$160 in additional revenue from existing customers alone.

5. Repeat-Customer Behavior

79% of customers return — and they drive 88% of revenue

Of 19 customers, 15 (79%) placed multiple orders. These repeat buyers generated $3,285 (80% of total revenue) vs. just $809 from one-time buyers.

The top repeat buyer, C009, spent $877 across 3 orders — all in the Office category (Standing Desk, Ergonomic Chair, Bookshelf). This single customer accounts for 21% of total revenue.

Key insight: Repeat customers don't just buy more often — they buy higher-value items. The average repeat customer spends $219 vs. $202 for one-time buyers, but the real gap is at the top: the top 5 repeat buyers alone account for 65% of all revenue. Customer C001 (5 orders, $271) shows the classic "frequent small purchases" pattern in Electronics, while C009 shows "infrequent big purchases" in Office furniture.

6. Actionable Recommendations

Five data-driven next steps
  1. 1
    Launch cross-category campaigns in "desert" regions. The South has never purchased Electronics or Home products. Targeted email campaigns with bundled offers (e.g., "Complete your home gym — add a Smart Plug to your Sports order") could unlock $160+ in incremental revenue from existing South customers.
  2. 2
    Increase AOV in the East region. With 14 orders but only $753 in revenue (AOV $54), the East is order-rich but value-poor. Introduce minimum-spend free-shipping thresholds at $75 to nudge basket size upward, and promote higher-ticket Electronics items that already perform well there.
  3. 3
    Protect the whale: create a VIP tier for C009 and similar high-spenders. Customer C009 alone drives 21% of revenue. A dedicated account manager, early access to new Office products, and volume discounts would protect this revenue stream and encourage the 4 other repeat customers spending $250+ to increase frequency.
  4. 4
    Convert the 4 one-time buyers into repeat customers. These customers spent $809 total — an average of $202 each. A post-purchase follow-up sequence with a 10% second-order discount could shift them into the repeat cohort, where average lifetime spend is $219 and growing.
  5. 5
    Investigate why Office revenue is absent from the East. With zero Office sales in 14 orders, the East is a complete blind spot for the highest-AOV category. Run a diagnostic: is it a product availability issue, a marketing gap, or genuine demand absence? A small test promotion could reveal whether this is a fixable gap or a structural market difference.
