Repeat Customer Behavior

Retention drives revenue. 15 of 19 customers (79%) have placed more than one order, and they contribute $3,285 — that's 80% of all revenue. The average repeat customer places 3.1 orders and spends $219 total.

Interestingly, one-time customers have a higher average order value ($202) than repeat customers ($72). This is because one-time buyers include customers who purchased high-ticket items like the Standing Desk but haven't returned yet — a clear opportunity to convert them into repeat buyers.

Meanwhile, Sports and Electronics each have 14 orders but generate less revenue. This reveals a volume vs. value tradeoff that's critical for inventory and marketing decisions.

Actionable Recommendations

  1. Convert high-value one-time buyers into repeat customers. The 4 one-time customers who spent $809 include buyers of premium items. Target them with personalized re-engagement campaigns: "Thanks for your purchase — here's 15% off your next order."
  2. Expand the Office category strategically. With the highest AOV ($123), Office is your profit engine. Add complementary lower-priced items (desk accessories, organizers) to increase order frequency without cannibalizing high-ticket sales.
  3. Launch a loyalty program for the 15 repeat customers. They drive 80% of revenue. A tiered system (e.g., free shipping after 3 orders, 10% back after 5) could increase order frequency from the current 3.1 orders per customer.
  4. Diagnose the Midwest's retention problem. With only 47.1% repeat revenue, this region is leaking customers. Send satisfaction surveys, analyze delivery times and product quality issues, and test a "second purchase discount" to re-engage one-time buyers.
  5. Double down on West region Electronics. The heatmap shows Electronics × West is your strongest category-region pairing. Consider regional promotions, faster shipping, or exclusive bundles to capitalize on proven demand.
  6. Balance the portfolio. While Office leads revenue, over-reliance on high-ticket items creates volatility. Invest in Sports and Electronics (both with 14 orders) to build a more predictable, diversified revenue base.
